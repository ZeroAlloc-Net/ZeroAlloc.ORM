# v2.x Sqlite benchmark results

**Status:** Captured 2026-10-15 at 2.3.1, from `main` at `026856b`.

> Allocations are exact. Speed is shown as same-run ratios, because the benchmark host is a shared
> VM; absolute timings are not published.

ZA.ORM is a **relative-position** tool: compare the rows of one table, never numbers across tables
or machines.

## Environment

```
BenchmarkDotNet v0.15.8, Linux Debian GNU/Linux 13 (trixie)
Intel Core Ultra 7 255H 3.69GHz, 1 CPU, 12 logical and 12 physical cores
.NET SDK 10.0.402
  [Host]     : .NET 10.0.13 (10.0.13, 10.0.1326.50504), X64 RyuJIT x86-64-v3
```

- The host is a KVM virtual machine on a shared machine, and other workloads ran during the capture.
- BenchmarkDotNet's default job ran out of process, with three launches per benchmark, so each ratio averages over three processes.
- The run was pinned to four vCPUs with `taskset -c 2-5`.
- The benchmark project uses server GC with concurrent GC.
- Packages: Microsoft.Data.Sqlite 10.0.12, SQLitePCLRaw.lib.e_sqlite3 3.50.3, Dapper 2.1.89 and Dapper.AOT 1.1.0.

## Reproduction

```bash
# From repo root
dotnet build -c Release tests/ZeroAlloc.ORM.Benchmarks
cd tests/ZeroAlloc.ORM.Benchmarks
taskset -c 2-5 dotnet run -c Release --no-build -- \
  --filter "ZeroAlloc.ORM.Benchmarks.SingleRowReadBench*" \
           "ZeroAlloc.ORM.Benchmarks.MultiRowReadBench*" \
           "ZeroAlloc.ORM.Benchmarks.MultiResultSetBench*" \
           "ZeroAlloc.ORM.Benchmarks.InsertBench*" \
  --job Default --launchCount 3
```

## How to read the tables

- **Ratio and RatioSD** are copied from BenchmarkDotNet. Ratio is each row's mean time divided by
  `HandWrittenAdoNet`'s, averaged over the three launches.
- **Per-launch ratio** is the same ratio computed from each launch's mean separately, to show how
  far it moved between processes.
- **Allocated** is exact: total bytes allocated divided by operations, from BenchmarkDotNet's GC
  counters. BenchmarkDotNet's own summary rounds this to KB.
- **Alloc Ratio** is each row's allocation divided by `HandWrittenAdoNet`'s.

## SingleRowReadBench

_Workload:_ `SELECT Id, CustomerId, Total FROM Orders WHERE Id = @id`, 1 row materialised to `OrderRow`.

| Method            | Ratio | RatioSD | Per-launch ratio | Allocated | Alloc Ratio |
|------------------ |------:|--------:|-----------------:|----------:|------------:|
| HandWrittenAdoNet |  1.01 |    0.13 | 1.00             |   1,232 B |        1.00 |
| ZeroAlloc_ORM     |  1.10 |    0.14 | 1.06 – 1.25      |   1,568 B |        1.27 |
| Dapper_AOT        |  1.26 |    0.14 | 1.22 – 1.40      |   1,800 B |        1.46 |

ZA.ORM is about 10% slower than hand-written ADO.NET and allocates 336 B more per read. Dapper.AOT
is about 26% slower and allocates 568 B more.

## MultiRowReadBench

_Workload:_ 1000-row `SELECT … ORDER BY Id` drained into `List<OrderRow>`.

| Method            | Ratio | RatioSD | Per-launch ratio | Allocated   | Alloc Ratio |
|------------------ |------:|--------:|-----------------:|------------:|------------:|
| Dapper_AOT        |  0.90 |    0.06 | 0.88 – 0.97      | 130,000 B ¹ |      1.47 ¹ |
| ZeroAlloc_ORM     |  0.99 |    0.06 | 0.97 – 1.05      |    88,592 B |        1.00 |
| HandWrittenAdoNet |  1.00 |    0.09 | 1.00             |    88,200 B |        1.00 |

ZA.ORM is at time parity with hand-written ADO.NET and allocates 392 B more for 1000 rows, 0.4%.
Dapper.AOT is about 10% faster and allocates 47% more.

¹ BenchmarkDotNet's summary shows `-` for Dapper_AOT because its third launch read a total of 0
bytes despite 28 gen0, 2 gen1 and 1 gen2 collections. The first two launches each measured exactly
130,000 B per operation; the table uses that figure and the ratio derived from it.

## MultiResultSetBench

_Workload:_ `SELECT head; SELECT lines`, the head and 10 lines as a `(Order, IReadOnlyList<OrderLine>)` tuple.

| Method            | Ratio | RatioSD | Per-launch ratio | Allocated | Alloc Ratio |
|------------------ |------:|--------:|-----------------:|----------:|------------:|
| HandWrittenAdoNet |  1.00 |    0.05 | 1.00             |   2,680 B |        1.00 |
| ZeroAlloc_ORM     |  1.04 |    0.04 | 1.03 – 1.05      |   3,400 B |        1.27 |
| Dapper_AOT        |  1.24 |    0.05 | 1.21 – 1.26      |   4,072 B |        1.52 |

ZA.ORM is within 5% of hand-written ADO.NET; at v0.7.0 it was 30% slower. Dapper.AOT now runs this
workload, where at v0.7.0 it produced NA.

## InsertBench

_Workload:_ `INSERT INTO Orders (Id, CustomerId, Total) VALUES (@id, @cust, @total)` returning rows affected.

| Method            | Ratio | RatioSD | Per-launch ratio | Allocated | Alloc Ratio |
|------------------ |------:|--------:|-----------------:|----------:|------------:|
| HandWrittenAdoNet |  1.01 |    0.11 | 1.00             |   1,208 B |        1.00 |
| ZeroAlloc_ORM     |  1.17 |    0.13 | 1.09 – 1.27      |   1,856 B |        1.54 |
| Dapper_AOT        |  1.38 |    0.31 | 1.23 – 1.64      |   1,888 B |        1.56 |

ZA.ORM is about 17% slower than hand-written ADO.NET and allocates 648 B more per insert. At v0.7.0
it allocated 4% more than hand-written; that gap has grown. Dapper.AOT is slower again and
allocates about the same as ZA.ORM.

## Takeaways

- **Multi-row reads (1000 rows):** ZA.ORM is at time parity with hand-written ADO.NET and allocates 0.4% more. Dapper.AOT is about 10% faster but allocates 47% more.
- **Multi-result-set:** ZA.ORM is within 5% of hand-written, down from 30% at v0.7.0.
- **Single-row reads and inserts:** ZA.ORM sits between hand-written ADO.NET and Dapper.AOT on both time and allocation, at 10% and 17% slower than hand-written. These two ratios moved the most between launches.
- **Allocation:** ZA.ORM allocates more than hand-written on every single-statement workload: 336 B per read and 648 B per insert.
